# Entertainment motion validation

## Reference scope

The public LINE GAME promotional materials were reviewed as visual and information-design references, not as assets to reproduce. The [LINE Bubble 2 event page](https://linegame-official.blog.jp/archives/63437100.html) shows a large central character with smaller supporting characters; its text connects daily missions with tickets and stage completion with rewards. The [LINE Pokopoko event page](https://linegame-official.blog.jp/archives/63163802.html) uses prominent outlined headings and expressive characters, while its reward banner separates the reward from its conditions and deadline. These observations are from still images and page text. The [official 15-second promotion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XRfgW55q_gk) was identified, but video playback did not load, so its motion, transitions and rhythm were not observed.

The independent game film uses its own fictional character, event and graphics; it does not use LINE logos or character assets. The transferable design question is whether the viewer can identify the character, goal, reward and condition, not whether a particular branded layout is copied.

## Game event

The local 12-second `starfall-run-v2.mp4` has SHA-256 `5e818d28694ab348f29a723b72a9a1bb270efad78abaae4f7c07061366d97803`. It introduces a fictional courier, asks viewers to collect three stars, and connects the condition to a cloak and 500 gems. An independent reviewer inspected decoded frames at 270×480: the goal and reward condition are more legible than in v1, no sampled overlap or clipping was observed, and the event is labelled fictional. The reviewer also noted that small supporting text may still be difficult in an actual phone interface. Earlier feedback about the route, goal label and cloak shape led to specific changes rather than a style-only pass.

The MP4 contains H.264 and AAC and decoded without errors. The frame diagnostic reports **FAIL** for frames 191–269: the reward and condition are held on screen for reading. That status is retained, not relabelled as a pass; sampled stills do not establish how the hold feels at playback speed. Flash and the revised brief checker passed, seam reported WARN for missing motion data, and narration was skipped.

A separate creative run requested the `gpt-6-astra` model with high reasoning effort and produced another fictional event, `moonpost-final.mp4` (SHA-256 `cba66cf6cd1ea5511e69e4f9102b5fbc9f8fad909103a86bca7101405f921f8f`; 10 seconds, 720×1280). The runtime did not expose the actual model ID, so only the requested configuration is known. Its author revised an initial saved render to clarify collecting three letters and receiving 300 units; the parent independently inspected a sheet decoded from the final MP4. Selected-frame audit, frame, flash and brief checks passed; seam was WARN and narration SKIP. An unprivileged whole-engine test attempt had IPC/localhost and Blender failures; it is not evidence of a passing full suite. The AAC-to-authored-WAV correlation was 0.999711, not a listening or perceptual-sync test.

## Music promotion

The local 12-second `neon-tide-final-v2.mp4` has SHA-256 `68db4626924c760d22095512efc3757e0f03a6a459457dcd1cba4a06fd1562bc`. Its original local 120 BPM test track has a half-second beat; title changes at 3, 6 and 10 seconds were compared with the decoded PCM time axis. The final rendered audio correlated 0.99818 with the authored WAV after AAC decoding; this is signal evidence, not perceptual synchronization. An independent saved-frame review observed a clearer final title than v1 and no sampled text/wave overlap. It also found that those three changes were hard cuts and reused a similar circular-wave layout, leaving transition variety and continuity limited.

Frame and flash diagnostics passed, seam reported WARN for missing motion data, and narration was skipped. The original brief checker incorrectly treated the requested 10–15 second range as exactly 15 seconds and failed; the revised range checker passed the same retained must clause. The MP4 and AAC decoded without errors.

An independent review found the v2 changes at 3, 6 and 10 seconds were hard cuts with similar layouts. A v3b revision carried an expanding circle across the 3-second cut, but decoded frames 94–96 showed its line crossing the title despite a clean audit. Local v4 (SHA-256 `085618656b1ceaec9364a5ac182e832a659d96aec6e5558558b394b5028aa036`) masks the line out of the title area while retaining the transition on either side. The author inspected all saved frames 90–98; an independent inspection of selected saved frames confirmed that the earlier crossing was absent and the 89–90 shape transition remained. The other two transitions remain hard cuts. The v4 frame, flash and revised brief checks passed; seam was WARN and narration SKIP. No playback or listening was performed.

## Limits

These are local experiments, not a benchmark across models or proof of professional-quality equivalence. Neither production was inspected through actual normal-speed playback or direct listening; the stills, mobile-size downsampling and waveform measurements do not establish perceived rhythm, phone-screen legibility, audio quality or sync. The films were produced through collaboration agents, not a verified fresh Codex app session. Model identity for the game and music production was not exposed to those agents. Existing global Frameforge skills pointed to a separate checkout, so this work does not prove that the latest plugin is installed or automatically loaded in a new Codex session. Artifacts remain local and are identified by hashes above.

An independently prompted collaboration agent also created the same factual football brief without seeing prior output. It actually called the current MCP `make_motion_film` prompt, `read_skill`, and `film_new`, and produced a local MP4 (SHA-256 `585507a6d65d110611005bc83f4a7d8e82caad9238fe5ba8e566f508ad1f82e8`). A separate saved-frame review found correct sampled score states but repeated the same scoreboard composition for all three goals and replaced it with a separate result card. This shows that exposing the workflow is not proof that an agent will produce reference-level continuity. It was not an independent Codex app session.

For the earlier v10 football revision, downsampled 360-pixel stills retained the goal times; at 270 pixels the times were very small. Local v11 (SHA-256 `5aab1eefc93000c4c0dba86860059b0353552f9609ceee728ee9294ed0e020f3`) enlarges the time text and nodes. An independent comparison of saved MP4 frames at 270×480 found the times easier to distinguish with the sampled totals intact. Closer node spacing is a tradeoff, and device playback remains unverified.
