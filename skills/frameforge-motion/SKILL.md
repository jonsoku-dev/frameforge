# Motion graphics

Design the visual language and pace for the brief. Typography, graphics, images and information can all carry a film; a 3D object, dense effects, or constant motion is not required. Choose media and tools that serve the subject and respect the user's constraints.

Read `../frameforge-best-practices/references/bespoke.md` for rendering helpers and the relevant audio or 3D skill when needed. Existing examples and assets can explain APIs; do not copy their visual identity as a substitute for a concept.

## Workflow

1. Choose new or revise from the request. For a new film, use `frameforge new <slug>` or the repository template, set the requested duration, dimensions and fps, and fill `brief.md` with the verbatim request, clause-by-clause intent ledger, engine translation and gaps. For a revision, read the existing brief, plan, entry, relevant source and specified saved output first; preserve the original request and history, merge the new requirements and decisions into the existing brief, and preserve user-owned assets without recreating the film. When the user explicitly changes an earlier condition, record the supersession and follow the latest instruction. Run `brief-check` and report unresolved conflicts.
2. In `prompt.md`, describe the concept, visual world, palette and chosen media. For each major event or shot, plan its frame range, information before and after, what remains visible, what becomes the focus, the motion that connects or separates the states, and the settled reading interval. Give events meaningfully different emphasis where their roles differ. For factual sequences, record the source and make one event/state table the authority for the implementation: associate each event with its subject, time, resulting state and frame, and distinguish viewpoints or totals that use different ordering. Check the displayed state immediately before, during and after each event against that table; check the settled result separately. A cut or hold can be intentional; don't add motion just to fill a frame.
3. For a new film, write `Spot.tsx` as a pure function of `useCurrentFrame()` using the chosen media and Frameforge components. For a revision, make the smallest necessary changes to the existing relevant files; do not replace `Spot.tsx` by default. Keep changed frame logic deterministic, using seeded randomness rather than `Math.random` or wall-clock time. Choose type and assets appropriate to the brief and record their origin and rights.
4. Use requested or licensed sound, or generate a score when appropriate. Associate each cue with its intended visual event and frame, including the main movement and settled state. When a cue or event moves, update the plan to match the implemented timing and inspect the final rendered mix within the authorized scope using available evidence. Measurements can locate a cue but do not establish perceived synchronization; verify the actual mix when listening is available. Do not replace a requested track or use external services without authorization.
5. Within the authorized scope, inspect relevant states, transitions and the last requested frame. Before rendering a contact sheet or still, check whether asset generation, network requests or other out-of-range effects are permitted; selecting frames alone does not prevent them. If this route is not allowed or cannot be assessed, use an authorized observation method or mark the affected observations NOT VERIFIED. Critique the observed frames against the planned information hierarchy and reading intervals; identify the weakest scenes and revise their composition, action or timing. If a reference and candidate are available, identify the exact artifacts and compare corresponding narrative events, sampling before, during and after consequential transitions. Record which element leads the eye, what information changes, what carries through, and why the change helps or hinders understanding; use the finding to propose a specific revision. Do not copy a reference's factual errors or treat a reference's style as mandatory. Inspect the revision at the same events using an authorized method. Repeat for concrete unresolved defects when evidence and permissions allow; a score or a sheet alone is not a quality verdict.
6. Check the render's possible effects and authorization, then render the requested range (or the full film if authorized) and run `frameforge check <out.mp4> --film <slug>`. If no authorized render path is available, report the affected result as NOT VERIFIED. A partial render does not establish observations outside its range. Review flagged intervals against their purpose and the brief: retain useful reading holds, correct unintended stagnation and technical defects, and report the actual check status and any unresolved conflict. A skipped or unavailable check is not a pass. Inspect canvas and shader text visually because the audit cannot see it.
7. Watch the authorized final output at playback speed and listen to its mix when available. Review transitions, focus, reading time, ending and event synchronization. If a separate reviewer is available, provide the brief and actual render and address concrete findings; otherwise label self-review. A contact sheet, audio waveform or automated pass is not playback or listening. Record observed intervals, findings, corrections and anything not verified.
8. Report the output, every must and forbid clause as MET, UNMET or NOT VERIFIED with evidence, actual check results, gaps and substitutes, and the playback and listening performed. Do not claim a creative or audio result that was not observed.
